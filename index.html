The Maison

A house built where the light meets the water.

Van Tee & Arke began with a single observation: a stone is never the same twice. Held to a window above the harbour, a diamond gathers the grey of the morning, the blue of the afternoon and the gold of the last hour of light. We built the house around that changing light — choosing stones for how they live rather than how they shine in a case, and designing every piece to be read in motion, on a body, in a room.

The House Code

Three things we do not compromise

01

Light before ornament

A piece is designed for how it behaves as you move and as the day changes — never for how much it can show while standing still.

02

The stone leads

Design begins only once the stone is chosen. The setting exists to serve it, hold it safely, and then disappear against the skin.

03

One room, one guest

The salon receives a single client at a time. Nothing is shown in a hurry, and nothing is asked of you but your attention.

Savoir-Faire

From rough stone to light

Four stages, one pair of hands. From the first sketch to the final polish, a piece is never passed around.

Stage 01

Selection

A gemmologist examines every stone under 10× magnification for colour, clarity and fluorescence — one is kept in a hundred.

Stage 02

Drawing

The designer paints a 1:1 gouache study on grey card, fixing the direction of every ray of light before a gram of metal is cut.

Stage 03

Forging

The goldsmith bends, solders and files the setting by hand so it hugs the skin and all but disappears.

Stage 04

Setting

The setter raises tiny gold beads with a graver to hold each stone, working to a tolerance finer than a hair.

The Salon

Twenty-two floors above the harbour, in a room that changes colour with the sky.

Where every stone is finally judged — in daylight, against the water.

Our salon sits high above Victoria Harbour. Marble vitrines, flowers renewed each week, and a wall of glass that turns the whole room silver at dawn and amber at dusk. Pieces are brought to the window, not the lamp; a stone that only lives under a spotlight never leaves the bench. You are received one at a time, seated, and given the room for as long as you need it.
